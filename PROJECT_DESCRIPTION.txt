EcoReceipt — Project Description
================================

Three versions of the same pitch, at increasing levels of detail.
Pick whichever fits the slot you're filling.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
SIMPLE (elevator pitch, ~30 seconds)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

EcoReceipt turns a photo of your grocery receipt into a carbon
footprint.

Snap the receipt, and we read every line item, estimate the emissions
behind each one, and show you the total in terms you actually feel —
like the number of miles you'd have to drive to match it. Then we tell
you the handful of swaps that would cut it the most.

Most people want to shop greener but have no idea which items in their
cart actually matter. EcoReceipt answers that in about five seconds,
using a receipt they were already holding.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
MEDIUM (~150 words, for a submission form or landing page)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

EcoReceipt is a web app that estimates the carbon footprint of your
groceries from a photo of your receipt.

You upload or snap a receipt and EcoReceipt reads it, identifies each
product, and calculates the emissions attached to it. You get a total
footprint, a per-item breakdown showing exactly which purchases
dominate, and a real-world equivalent — the miles of driving your trip
is worth — so the number means something.

The part that changes behaviour is eco-swaps: for each high-impact
item, EcoReceipt suggests a lower-carbon alternative and shows the
savings, ranked so the biggest wins come first. Swapping ground beef
for lentils, for example, is a far larger cut than anything else in a
typical cart.

Every trip is saved to a history, and a progress view tracks your
footprint across recent shops, so improvement is visible over time.
No manual data entry, no spreadsheets, no guessing.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
DETAILED (full pitch / demo script / devpost writeup)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

THE PROBLEM

Food is responsible for roughly a quarter of global greenhouse gas
emissions, and household grocery shopping is where most people have
the most direct, most frequent influence over it. The problem isn't
willingness — it's information.

Emissions are wildly uneven across a cart, and the distribution is
invisible at the shelf. A kilogram of beef carries around thirty times
the emissions of a kilogram of lentils. Two carts that cost the same
can differ enormously in footprint. Nothing at the point of purchase
tells you which is which, and existing carbon calculators demand that
you type in every item by hand — which nobody sustains past the first
week.

The information exists. It just never reaches the person deciding.


WHAT ECORECEIPT DOES

EcoReceipt removes the data entry entirely. You give it the receipt
you already have, and it does the rest.

  1. Snap or upload a photo of a grocery receipt.
  2. EcoReceipt reads the receipt and extracts the line items —
     including the abbreviated, half-mangled product names that real
     receipts are full of.
  3. Each item is matched to an emissions factor and scaled by
     quantity and typical weight.
  4. You get a result: total footprint, per-item breakdown ranked by
     impact, confidence indicators, and a driving-distance equivalent.
  5. You get eco-swaps — concrete lower-carbon substitutes for your
     worst offenders, ranked by how much carbon each would actually
     save.

There's also manual entry for anyone without a receipt on hand, or for
planning a shop before making it.


HOW IT WORKS

The hard problem is identification. Receipts don't say "organic whole
wheat bread" — they say "ORG BRD WHL WHT". Turning that into an
emissions number reliably is the core of the system.

We solve it with a layered resolution chain, so that a miss at one
level is caught by the next:

  - Reading the receipt. A vision model converts the photo straight
    into structured line items. No separate OCR engine, no
    preprocessing pipeline, and it handles crumpled receipts, odd
    lighting, and retailer-specific abbreviations far better than
    traditional text extraction.

  - Primary matching. Each identified item is looked up against a
    live emissions database, so footprints reflect a maintained
    real-world dataset rather than a hardcoded guess.

  - Offline fallback. A curated local dataset of common grocery items,
    with fuzzy keyword matching, catches anything the live lookup
    can't identify. This also means the core experience keeps working
    with no network and no API keys — which matters for a demo, and
    matters more for reliability.

  - Last-resort estimation. Anything still unmatched gets a model-
    generated estimate, and failing that, a conservative average
    grocery factor.

The pipeline never blocks and never dead-ends. An unrecognized item
degrades to a lower-confidence estimate instead of breaking the
receipt, and confidence is surfaced in the result rather than hidden.

Eco-swap recommendations come from the curated dataset, where each
high-impact item is mapped to a lower-carbon substitute in the same
role. Suggestions only appear when the swap genuinely saves carbon,
and they're ordered by size of saving, so the first thing you read is
the thing most worth doing.


MAKING THE NUMBER MEAN SOMETHING

"14.2 kg CO2e" is not a number most people can act on. Every total is
also expressed as an equivalent driving distance, using the standard
EPA per-mile figure. "This trip is worth 78 miles of driving" lands in
a way the raw figure doesn't.

Trips are saved automatically to a history you can browse, with a
read-only detail view for any past shop. A progress view charts your
footprint across recent trips, turning one-off curiosity into
something you can actually watch improve.


TECHNICAL NOTES

React and TypeScript on the front end, a Python API on the back, and
SQLite for saved trips.

Two things we're quietly proud of:

  - Graceful degradation is designed in, not bolted on. Every external
    dependency has a fallback, and the app is fully demoable with zero
    API keys configured.

  - History storage is swappable behind a single contract. The app
    runs against browser storage or a real backend without the pages
    or the progress calculations knowing which is which — and when a
    user moves from one to the other, their existing trips migrate
    atomically, preserving original timestamps, with old links still
    resolving afterward. Nothing is deleted until the migration is
    confirmed complete.


WHY IT MATTERS

The gap between wanting to shop sustainably and knowing how to isn't a
motivation gap, it's a feedback gap. People get no signal about the
carbon consequences of their choices, so those choices never change.

EcoReceipt closes that loop with the lowest-friction input possible: a
receipt you're already holding, a photo you already know how to take.
It turns an abstract global problem into a specific, personal, ranked
list of things you could do differently next week — and then shows you
whether you did.
